## 3. Enterprise-to-equity bridge check

The bridge identity is:

`Equity value = Enterprise value - debt - other debt-like claims + cash + non-operating investments`

Using only Oracle's disclosed cash and gross principal debt, the preliminary adjustment is:

`Equity value = Enterprise value - $130,105m + $31,289m = Enterprise value - $98,816m`

If the $605 million of marketable securities is confirmed to be non-operating and included as cash-like, the adjustment becomes:

`Equity value = Enterprise value - $130,105m + $31,289m + $605m = Enterprise value - $98,211m`

This arithmetic verifies the direction of the bridge: debt lowers equity value and cash/non-operating investments raise it. ## 5. Known evidence, consequential assumptions, and unknowns

### Known evidence

- Oracle generated $67,357 million of fiscal 2026 revenue, including $18,101 million of cloud-infrastructure revenue.
- Operating cash flow was $31,977 million, capital expenditures were $55,663 million, and free cash flow was negative $23,686 million.
- Cash and cash equivalents were $31,289 million; gross principal payments for all borrowings totaled $130,105 million.
- Fiscal 2026 diluted weighted-average shares were 2,914 million, and diluted EPS attributable to common shareholders was $5.83.
- The 10-K states that Oracle expects the upward capital-expenditure trend to continue in fiscal 2027 and following years as it expands data-center capacity.

### Consequential assumptions

- Cloud-infrastructure demand remains durable rather than reflecting a temporary AI-capacity shortage.
- New data centers achieve sufficient utilization and pricing to earn returns above Oracle's cost of capital.
- Cloud margins improve enough to offset depreciation, power, financing, and operating costs associated with the infrastructure buildout.
- Oracle can refinance or repay debt without materially impairing common-equity value.
- Stock-based compensation and other dilution remain manageable on a per-share basis.

### Unknowns

- ORCL's current share price, market capitalization, and enterprise value on the valuation date.
- The amount and timing of contracted cloud backlog converting to recognized revenue and cash flow.
- Normalized maintenance capital expenditure versus growth capital expenditure.
- Incremental returns on the fiscal 2026-2027 data-center investment cycle.
- Customer concentration, contract cancellation protections, power availability, and data-center utilization.
- A fully adjusted net-debt figure that reconciles carrying values, principal, leases, preferred stock, and other debt-like or non-operating items.
- The normalized diluted share count after considering unvested awards, options, performance awards, and mandatory convertible preferred stock.
